# References and their precise role

Harvard style. Primary-source metadata and substantive relevance checked 9 November 2026. These works provide conceptual context or published empirical evidence **about their own samples**, not calibration or validation of LVCS’s hypothetical numbers. No paper below supplies the $9.17m shortfall, $114.65m budget threshold, policy wealth figures, 50% survival result or constructed return paths.

## Ang, Papanikolaou and Westerfield (2014)

Ang, A., Papanikolaou, D. and Westerfield, M.M. (2014) ‘Portfolio Choice with Illiquid Assets’, *Management Science*, 60(11), pp. 2737–2761. [DOI:10.1287/mnsc.2014.1986](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1986).

**Supports:** liquidity constraints can matter for portfolio choice beyond an endpoint return comparison. **Limit:** dynamic optimal-allocation model with uncertain trading opportunities; not LVCS’s call schedule or stress experiment. **Verification:** final publisher article metadata/abstract checked, with original author-university record corroboration. The earlier archived NBER paper is a 2013 draft, not the final journal version. Attribution: Slides 6 and technical caption; methodology section on scope.

## CalPERS (2024)

California Public Employees’ Retirement System (CalPERS) (2024) *Trust Level Review: As of June 30, 2024*. September. [Official report](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/sites/default/files/spf/docs/forms-publications/trust-level-review-june-30-2024.pdf), PDF p.56, liquidity section/exhibits 8.17–8.18.

**Supports:** institutional liquidity assessment considers dated sources and uses, with resources beyond our segregated model account. **Limit:** one institution, a shorter liquidity horizon and a broader balance sheet; it neither prescribes the model’s 10% buffer nor validates the $100m example as representative. **Verification:** original official 69-page PDF preserved in the earlier package; title/date and liquidity section reinspected. The release direct-link check returned200; an earlier web-reader request returned403, so access can vary by client. Attribution: companion note only; no CalPERS return or policy parameter enters the artwork.

## Gourier, Phalippou and Westerfield (2024)

Gourier, E., Phalippou, L. and Westerfield, M.M. (2024) ‘Capital Commitment’, *The Journal of Finance*, 79(5), pp. 3407–3457. [DOI:10.1111/jofi.13382](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13382). [Author’s final-publication record and abstract](https://markwesterfield.com/papers/GPW-Capital-Commitment.html).

**Supports:** LP commitment obligations and the economic relevance of commitment management. **Limit:** structural model and welfare analysis; its findings are not LVCS’s accounting results. Its emphasis on quantity uncertainty cautions against using our selected timing example to claim a large universal premium. **Verification:** final citation, DOI and author abstract checked. The archived author PDF with “Final” in its filename bears an April2023 cover; no draft numerical table is treated as final2024 evidence. Attribution: Slides1 and 6, technical caption and methodology.

## Institutional Limited Partners Association (2017)

Institutional Limited Partners Association (ILPA) (2017) *Subscription Lines of Credit and Alignment of Interests: Considerations and Best Practices for Limited and General Partners*. June. [Official original guidance](https://ilpa.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/ILPA-Subscription-Lines-of-Credit-and-Alignment-of-Interests-June-2017.pdf).

**Supports:** the distinction between fund borrowing and LP resources; delayed calls, uncalled-commitment security and exposure/disclosure implications. **Limit:** dated industry guidance, not binding law, a claim of universal facility use or current market prevalence. No ILPA numerical lending limit is imported into the model. **Verification:** original7-page document read, especially PDF pp.2–3 and footnote3; official resource record confirms June2017. Attribution: technical caption and methodology financing section; Slide 2 points to that note.

## Robinson and Sensoy (2016)

Robinson, D.T. and Sensoy, B.A. (2016) ‘Cyclicality, performance measurement, and cash flow liquidity in private equity’, *Journal of Financial Economics*, 122(3), pp. 521–543. [DOI:10.1016/j.jfineco.2016.09.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2016.09.008). [Primary university publication record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1060726).

**Supports:** calls and distributions have market-related and fund-age components; receipt timing matters for liquidity analysis. **Limit:** published historical buyout/VC sample, not a present-day US-VC-only calibration; it does not establish that calls always surge during downturns. No empirical coefficient or schedule is imported. **Verification:** final title, authors,2016 publication, volume/pages and DOI checked against the preserved final university record; live record retrieved once, with subsequent requests timing out. The archived2011 NBER draft is separately identified. Attribution: methodology context, not as the source of the selected early/late paths.

## Sørensen and Jagannathan (2015)

Sørensen, M. and Jagannathan, R. (2015) ‘The Public Market Equivalent and Private Equity Performance’, *Financial Analysts Journal*, 71(4), pp. 43–50. [DOI:10.2469/faj.v71.n4.4](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v71.n4.4).

**Supports:** private cash-flow performance measurement must specify the investor’s public opportunity set and valuation assumptions. **Limit:** the paper provides an asset-pricing foundation for PME under its assumptions; LVCS’s deterministic whole-budget wealth comparison is not a newly estimated PME or risk-adjusted return. **Verification:** final publisher metadata, abstract and issue record checked. The publisher site lists an online-platform date in2018 for the2015 issue; the bibliographic year is2015. Attribution: companion methodology measurement section only.
