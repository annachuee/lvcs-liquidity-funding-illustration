A strong terminal return cannot fund a capital call if the money is unavailable when it is due.

In private funds, the general partner (GP) calls capital; the limited partner (LP) must provide it.

Our hypothetical LP starts with a $100m liquid budget and a $100m commitment funded by selling public assets. We reorder the same 14 annual returns, including one −40% interval, preserving approximately 8% annual compounded performance.

With the loss in year 2, the model has a $9.17m funding shortfall in year 4. With the loss in year 12, every baseline call is funded.

Borrowing, outside top-ups and other institutional resources are excluded. Real LPs may have additional liquidity arrangements: these results are not estimates of institutional default probabilities.

Methods and references: https://github.com/annachuee/lvcs-liquidity-funding-illustration.
